# Medieval building principles (research notes, 2026-11-15)

What the generators must do, learned from building guides (sources at the bottom). These apply to every
faction; faction style guides add their own palette and signature details.

## 1. Silhouette first (the biggest lesson from gallery v1: "all houses too similar")
- Different **building types**, not one box with swapped add-ons: cottage (low walls, tall roof, dormers),
  townhouse (narrow, tall, jettied, gable to the street), L-house (main block + wing with a cross gable),
  longhouse (long, eaves to the street, dormers), saltbox (gable with a long catslide over a lean-to),
  house with a small tower or stair turret.
- **Roof forms:** gable, half-hipped (gable below, hipped above), hipped, saltbox/catslide, cross gable,
  dormers. Roofs steep (45°). Bigger footprints non-rectangular (L or T).
- Roof sections at different heights; avoid excessive symmetry.

## 2. Depth (called the most important fundamental)
- Roof overhang of one block, with upside-down stairs under it as rafters/trim.
- Door set into the wall; window reveals and sills; glass panes instead of glass blocks.
- Jettied upper floors with brackets; beam ends sticking out; support posts and fences.
- Depth should serve the structure, not decorate for its own sake.

## 3. Details
- Shutters from trapdoors, varied (side, open/closed) rather than identical on every window.
- Flower boxes and planters under windows; overhangs over windows and doors; balconies; porches.
- Chimneys with smoke (campfire); lanterns used sparingly.

## 4. Variation and weathering
- No long unbroken runs of one block. Roofs: at least two wood types, darker where water runs off (eaves),
  lighter on exposed ridges.
- Stone weathering (mossy, cracked) clustered where water and wear go: corners, bases, around openings.
  Avoid random "smallpox" speckle and horizontal "sandwich" stripes.
- Mix materials for contrast: stone base, dark timber, light infill, a different wood on the roof.

## 5. Interior and yard
- Chairs from stairs, tables from slabs and fences, barrels, bookshelves; rafters visible inside.
- Landscaping: paths of mixed blocks, gardens, hedges, small trees, wells, woodpiles.
